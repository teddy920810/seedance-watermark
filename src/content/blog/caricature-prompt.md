You ask ChatGPT to caricature a photo of your friend. The colors look great, the grin looks right, and somehow the face belongs to a stranger. Before you regenerate, look at the original again. Would you recognize your friend by the eyebrows, the shape of the glasses, or the way one corner of the mouth lifts? The best detail to exaggerate is often the one that already gives the face away.

Upload a photo you have permission to edit. Choose two or three traits that should remain familiar, describe one trait to push further, and make one version you like before decorating it for a holiday. The prompts below take that approach from blank-background portrait to an avatar, birthday invitation, or seasonal card. Keep the original photo available so you can compare each edit with the same source.

## Start with a photo that shows the face clearly

![Source photograph of a smiling fictional adult with round glasses, strong eyebrows, and visible jacket collar.](/uploads/blog/caricature-prompt/section-1.jpg)

*Source photograph of a smiling fictional adult with round glasses, strong eyebrows, and visible jacket collar.*

Choose a front-facing or slightly angled photo with even light across the eyes and cheeks. A crop from the upper chest up gives you room for glasses, hair and the collar without pulling attention toward clothing. If a person usually wears their glasses, take a photo where you can see their eyes through the lenses. Strong glare can make the eyewear stand out while hiding part of the expression you wanted to keep.

Write down two or three details you can point to. Suppose the photo shows a smiling person with thin round glasses, prominent straight eyebrows, and a slight gap between the front teeth. Those details give you a clearer brief than “make me a funny cartoon.” If just one characteristic needs exaggeration, choose the one that contributes best to the expression. For this example, it might be the eyebrow lift. You can keep the round glasses and tooth gap recognizable while giving the brow line a little more movement.

ChatGPT Images supports editing an uploaded image by describing what you want to change. Start with the clearest usable photo and ask for a simple background. Keep the subject's pose and look in the source visible. A big angle change can introduce details that the source never shows, which makes it harder to tell whether the edit captured the person's likeness. [OpenAI Images in ChatGPT Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11084440-images-in-chatgpt)

**Copyable starter prompt**

> Turn the person in my uploaded photo into a friendly hand-drawn caricature from the chest up. Preserve the round dark glasses, the visible tooth gap, and the uneven corners of the smile. Exaggerate the strong eyebrow line slightly. Keep the head angle, hair part, and blue jacket collar from the photo. Draw clean ink lines with a few flat paint colors and a quiet cream background. Leave clear space around the hair and shoulders.

Once you have the first portrait, compare it with the photo side by side. Cover the jacket area and see if the face still looks familiar. Cover the glasses and check the smile. The goal is to keep more than one cue intact so the result still reads clearly when you shrink it or change the background later.

## Adjust the exaggeration without losing the likeness

![Close view of three hand drawn caricature passes of the same fictional person, increasing exaggeration.](/uploads/blog/caricature-prompt/section-2.jpg)

*Close view of three hand drawn caricature passes of the same fictional person, increasing exaggeration.*

Try one small revision before changing the whole style. If the first image makes the grin too broad, ask to bring only the mouth corners back toward the original while retaining the new eyebrows. If the portrait feels stiff, choose a quality in the original expression that you can amplify. “Lift the existing left eyebrow a little farther” offers a concrete adjustment that you can compare with the old version.

**Prompt to tune the face**

> Revise the current caricature using the uploaded photo as the likeness reference. Restore the original width of the smile and the slight tooth gap. Keep the larger expressive eyebrows and the current round glasses. Preserve the head tilt, illustrated line quality, colors, and plain background. Spend this edit on the mouth and smile.

If your ChatGPT editor offers a selection, you can highlight the mouth area and describe the edit. OpenAI's help page says selection boundaries can be imprecise and changes can reach outside them. Compare the result with both the original photo and the draft before accepting it. If the glasses change shape during a mouth edit, return to the prior image and narrow the instruction or selection. [OpenAI Images in ChatGPT Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11084440-images-in-chatgpt)

For a larger change in style, start again from the source photo and keep the same description of the face. A newspaper-style pen drawing might use crisp black lines and a little crosshatching. A sticker might use broad contours and a few saturated flat colors. Repeat the details that matter in the new language. For a small sticker, the tooth gap might become a single purposeful dark mark. For an illustration in a card, you can leave more room for subtler marks and shadows.

Make your judgment at the final size as well as zoomed in. The large drawing can have appealing detail that disappears in a small social avatar. Crop a copy to a rough circle or square and check the strongest features again. If the result depends on a fine detail near the edge, bring the face closer to the middle or reinforce a more visible trait. Keep the version with the best likeness before trying another style so you have a clean way back.

For a second opinion, show the small draft to someone who knows the person and ask which details made them recognize the face. An open question such as “Who does this remind you of?” gives you more information than asking whether the drawing looks good. If they recognize only the jacket or the background, return to the face and restore a distinctive feature from the original photo.

## Adapt one approved face for a birthday or seasonal card

![Actual reference edit of the source portrait above. The round glasses, hoop earring, and head tilt carry into the autumn card.](/uploads/blog/caricature-prompt/section-3.jpg)

*Actual reference edit of the source portrait above. The round glasses, hoop earring, and head tilt carry into the autumn card.*

Save the face you already like as a clean portrait with a quiet backdrop. Use that image as the visual starting point when you ask for a holiday variation. Keep the wording about the face stable and change the surrounding objects, palette and available space. For a printed card, decide where you want the message before you generate. Keep the main subject out of that space so you can typeset or write the message afterward.

**Square avatar**

> Adapt the approved illustrated portrait for a square profile image. Center the head with enough margin for a circular crop. Keep the face, eyebrows, glasses, smile, and ink lines. Use a pale blue background with one flat color. Keep the image readable at a small size.

**Birthday invitation**

> Keep the approved caricature's face, glasses, eyebrow shape, and blue jacket. Place the portrait in the lower right of a vertical birthday invitation. Add two loosely drawn balloons and a small cake on a nearby table. Leave quiet cream space above and to the left for invitation details.

**Autumn card**

> Use the same approved caricature, with the same facial features and head angle. Add two or three copper leaves beside the shoulders and use a pale gold background. Keep the area around the glasses and hair clear. Leave the upper portion empty for a short handwritten greeting.

## Frequently asked questions

![A designer comparing a small caricature avatar with a printed source photo in daylight.](/uploads/blog/caricature-prompt/section-4.jpg)

*A designer comparing a small caricature avatar with a printed source photo in daylight.*

**Why does my caricature look like a different person?** Go back to a clear photo. Name two or three visible features you can compare, such as an asymmetrical smile, a distinct glasses shape and a clear hair part. Request a small exaggeration in one feature first. If the new version still gets several features wrong, choose a source photo that shows them more clearly or try a closer crop with softer light.

**How do I keep the face and change only the art style?** Upload the original photo, name the facial cues to preserve and describe what the new surface should look like. Ask for line quality, shading and color rather than a generic “different style.” If you edit a prior drawing, compare the new face against the photo as well as the drawing. That makes it easier to catch a small drift that carries forward from the previous pass.

**Can I reuse the same caricature prompt for different holidays?** Keep a saved portrait and a short list of likeness traits. Change the seasonal props, clothing if needed, and the amount of open room for text. If a prop overlaps the face or hair, move it into the background. Look at the new crop with the original open beside it. Check the glasses, eyebrows and smile before moving on to the greeting or placement.

**What if I need a group caricature?** Start with a clear group photo you have permission to use. Identify each person by their position in the photo and give only one or two distinguishing cues per person. For a small print where each person's face matters, ask for individual portraits and assemble them in your layout tool so one person's glasses or smile does not transfer to somebody else. Look over the final group at print size before committing.
