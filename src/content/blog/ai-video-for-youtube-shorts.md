Your generated garden shot looks lovely on a laptop. On your phone, the watering can disappears behind captions, the first few seconds say nothing, and the narration ends before the picture does. None of those problems requires a more cinematic prompt. They require an edit planned for a small vertical screen.

An AI video generator for YouTube Shorts can supply footage or help assemble a finished draft. Those are different jobs. Start by deciding which one you actually need, then build one short piece you can inspect from beginning to end.

## Quick Answer

Choose your workflow before generating: new visual clips, a narrated first draft, or highlights from an existing recording. Write one clear idea and map each line to a shot. Compose for vertical viewing, generate only the missing footage, and add narration and captions in an editor. Review the exported file on a phone. Follow YouTube's current disclosure and rights rules before uploading.

## Choose the right kind of Shorts tool

A cinematic generator makes visual material. It does not necessarily write a script, time captions, or create a complete story. A finished draft tool handles more of those stages. A repurposing tool starts with footage you already recorded.

| An idea or short script | Build a narrated draft and revise it | Invideo's Shorts workflow |

The [Invideo Shorts page](https://invideo.io/make/ai-youtube-shorts-generator/) describes a topic to draft route with scenes, voice, and captions. [OpusClip's page](https://www.opus.pro/tools/youtube-shorts-maker) starts with existing video and highlights. Neither category should be confused with generating one original camera shot from an image.

Use this comparison to avoid paying for the wrong job. If you have a good recording, you may need editing rather than synthetic footage. If you need an imaginary setting, a clipping tool cannot invent it simply by finding better moments in your recording.

## Build one Short from a clear shot plan

### Write the message before choosing the pictures

Write the useful point in one sentence. For example: look at the plant and soil before reaching for the watering can. Make sure any actual plant care advice you publish is appropriate for the species. A generated picture is an illustration, not evidence that a recommendation works.

Read the script aloud with a timer. Shorten the explanation before accelerating your voice. A manageable first project contains one observation, one action, and a clear ending. It does not need to use every second available on the platform.

Mark which lines need new footage. You might already have a real plant close up on your phone. Generate only an atmosphere shot or a transition that adds something useful. A folder full of attractive clips is less helpful than three clips attached to specific lines.

### Give every shot one job

Plan an opening that makes the subject understandable immediately. Follow it with the detail needed to explain the point. End with a result or a clear next action.

For the gardening exercise, the opening could show a drooping leaf, the middle a close view of the soil, and the ending the plant placed back on its shelf. Those are story beats, not proof of a horticultural diagnosis. Film the actual inspection if your explanation depends on a real condition.

Estimate how long each beat needs in the edit. Leave room for trimming, but avoid generating a long action just because the selector allows it. If the model struggles with fingers interacting with soil, change the shot or record that action yourself.

### Compose vertically from the start

Use a 9:16 composition when you want a full height vertical Short. Keep the important person, object, and action away from edges where controls or captions may compete for space. There is no single safe area diagram that replaces checking the current playback interface.

Avoid automatically cropping a wide group shot. The important person may leave the frame, or the crop may remove the very object your narration describes. Recompose the shot, choose a different view, or generate a vertical version deliberately.

Leave uncluttered space where you plan to place readable text later. Do not ask the video model to generate your exact captions inside the footage. Add and revise them as text in your editor.

### Request simple motion and inspect the result

Use an original prompt such as:

> Vertical close view of a potted herb on a bright kitchen shelf. One leaf moves gently in a light breeze. The camera stays still. Keep the pot shape, background, and light consistent. No cuts or generated lettering.

For image input, describe the motion needed rather than asking the model to redesign the reference. [Runway's image to video guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/48324313115155-Image-to-Video-Prompting-Guide) makes that distinction and recommends checking the source for artifacts.

Watch each output in motion. Check hands, faces, object shapes, movement direction, and the ending. Trim a clean portion when that serves the script. Reject a shot whose central action becomes misleading or impossible.

### Add narration and captions to the final edit

Record your own voice or use a voice you have permission to use. Edit the narration before producing final captions. Otherwise each audio revision can create a new timing problem.

Check names, numbers, and specialized words manually. Read the captions without listening. Then listen without reading. Both versions should communicate the same point.

Preview at phone size. Keep caption lines short enough to read comfortably and position them so they do not cover the useful action. If a caption must cross the plant or product to fit, reconsider its wording or move the shot rather than simply shrinking the text.

### Export and review the actual file

Watch the exported video, not only the editor preview. Check the first frame, transitions, audio level, captions, and final hold. An edit can look correct on the timeline while an export reveals a missing font, a clipped word, or a black ending.

This workflow gives you control over the relationship between an idea and its visuals. Its limitation is the time needed for review. Automated drafting can reduce assembly work, but it does not remove your responsibility to decide whether the finished video makes sense.

## Check length music and disclosure before uploading

[YouTube's official guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en) supports eligible square or vertical Shorts up to three minutes. That is a platform classification, not a recommendation to stretch every idea or a promise that your generator makes three continuous minutes.

The same guidance describes additional restrictions for Shorts longer than one minute with an active copyright claim. Choose audio with appropriate rights and confirm the track's actual usage conditions rather than assuming any song on your computer is suitable.

For realistic synthetic scenes, use the current [AI use disclosure guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en). YouTube distinguishes realistic, meaningful generation or alteration from clearly unrealistic material and minor production assistance. Check the examples that match your video; do not assume every AI assisted edit is treated identically.

Monetization is a separate decision. [YouTube's channel policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) address originality and inauthentic, repetitive, or mass produced content. A generator's download button cannot guarantee eligibility or audience growth.

## FAQ

### Can AI make a complete YouTube Short directly

Some tools produce an assembled draft from a script or topic. Check what the selected product includes, then review its facts, footage, captions, audio, and export conditions. A complete draft still needs your editorial approval before publication.

### How do I avoid captions covering the action

Plan clear space in the frame, add captions in an editor, and preview the exported video on a phone. Reposition text or change shots when it covers the detail viewers need. Do not rely on one fixed safe area measurement forever.

### When should I disclose AI use

Follow YouTube's current examples for realistic generated or meaningfully altered content. A realistic scene that never happened can require disclosure. Routine caption help and clearly fantastical imagery can be treated differently. Review the rule that fits the actual finished material.

## Conclusion

Choose the tool category that matches your starting material. Then make one Short whose script, shots, voice, and captions agree. If an interaction cannot be generated accurately, simplify it or film it. The final phone review is where those separate ingredients become a video someone can follow.
