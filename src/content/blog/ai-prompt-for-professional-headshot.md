You have a good photo from a friend's birthday. Your face is sharp, you look relaxed, and there is enough space around your shoulders for a profile crop. The problem is the party banner right behind your head. Uploading that picture and asking for a “professional headshot” gives an image model a lot of freedom. It may change the banner, your jacket, your age, and the shape of your smile in one pass. If you start with a specific edit, you can see which change helped and keep the version that still looks like you.

## Quick Answer

![Soft window light and visible facial features make a usable source portrait.](/uploads/blog/ai-prompt-for-professional-headshot/section-1.jpg)

*Soft window light and visible facial features make a usable source portrait.*

The best AI prompt for a professional headshot follows a simple formula. Identify the uploaded photo as the reference, name one change, and list the details you want to keep. You can use the examples below in Gemini or ChatGPT with an image-editing feature. The real comparison is between each result and your original photo.

## Choose a source photo that can survive the crop

![A phone at eye level captures a clear source portrait beside a window.](/uploads/blog/ai-prompt-for-professional-headshot/section-2.jpg)

*A phone at eye level captures a clear source portrait beside a window.*

Find a recent picture where both eyes are visible and the face is in focus. A friend can take it near a window using a phone at eye level. Stand back far enough to leave space around your hair and shoulders. If you wear glasses every day, wearing them in the source photo gives the editor a better reference for their shape and position. Choose a picture you are allowed to upload and edit.

Check the source at two sizes. At full size, can you see the corners of the eyes, the edges of the glasses, and the outline of the hair? At profile size, can you recognize your expression? A wide group photo might look sharp on a phone screen and have very few usable pixels once you crop to a single face. A frame from the same day showing your head and shoulders will usually give you a more workable starting point.

Try a square crop on a copy before uploading. Leave space around the top and sides so the image works inside a circle. If your organization asks for a portrait-shaped file, keep a wider original and crop separately for each destination. Asking the image model to invent missing shoulder or hair detail can produce an image that looks fine as a thumbnail and awkward at full size.

Gemini supports editing uploaded images with a written request. ChatGPT Images supports the same general workflow and also offers a selection tool for edits that need a visible region. If both tools are available to you, choose one source, issue the same prompt once in each, and compare the crop and likeness before doing another round. Controls and model names shown in your account can change. [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14286560?co=GENIE.PlatformDesktop&hl=en) · [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11084440-chatgpt-images-faq)

**A starting prompt for a crop and background change**

> Use the uploaded portrait as the reference. Keep the same face, expression, glasses, hairstyle, and clothing. Replace the wall behind me with a soft warm-gray wall. Keep the original light direction. Frame the head and upper shoulders with enough room for a centered circular crop.

Zoom in around hair and glasses after the edit. A bright edge around the hair can reveal that the model treated part of the person as background. If the replacement bleeds into the face, try a simpler background color and keep the source crop for the first pass. Make the final square in your photo editor once the replacement looks natural.

## Pick a prompt for clothing, light, or texture

![Profile portraits on a screen show how a wardrobe and background change can leave the face recognizable.](/uploads/blog/ai-prompt-for-professional-headshot/section-3.jpg)

*Profile portraits on a screen show how a wardrobe and background change can leave the face recognizable.*

Use your photo's most distracting detail to decide what to do next. A bright shirt can dominate a thumbnail. Strong overhead light can make the eyes hard to see. A busy wall can compete with the head and shoulders. Each problem has a different fix, so start with the one that affects the intended use most.

**Change a shirt and keep the face stable**

> In the uploaded photo, replace only the patterned shirt below the neckline with a plain navy crewneck. Keep the current neckline height, shoulder position, and head angle. Preserve my expression, facial features, hairline, and the original lighting.

If your editor has a selection tool, select the area of the shirt you want to change. Leave a clear margin around the chin, the edges of the hair, and any jewelry you want to keep. The outline is guidance. OpenAI notes that a ChatGPT image edit can extend beyond the selected area, so compare both versions before accepting the new shirt. [OpenAI's image editor guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11084440-chatgpt-images-faq)

For a styled jacket or blazer, check where it should sit on the shoulders and what is visible under the lapels. If the model makes the jacket look rigid or too wide, use less specific styling language and keep the original collar line. Reopen the source photo for each retry if the previous result altered the jaw or shifted your posture.

**Lighten shadows around the eyes**

> Keep the face, skin tone, expression, and existing background in the uploaded portrait. Gently soften the dark overhead shadow around my eyes. Keep the lighter side of my face on the same side as the original. Preserve natural contrast around my nose, cheeks, and chin.

Look for consistency between the shadows on the face and the reflection in glasses. A strong bright reflection on one side and a shadow that suggests light comes from the other side can make the whole photo feel assembled. If the source is heavily backlit and the face is hard to see, take a fresh photo with the window in front of you. That small practical change gives you an original you can also use on its own.

**Retain skin texture while correcting the photo**

> Retain the skin texture, freckles, expression lines, and age shown in the uploaded headshot. Make a subtle white balance correction so the highlights look neutral. Keep the original light direction and the existing shape of my eyes, nose, lips, and jaw.

Words such as “glamorous” or “flawless” invite broad styling decisions. For a professional photo you need to recognize at a glance, ask for an observable correction and check the same area before and after. The prompt can name freckles as a detail to retain. The comparison tells you whether they survived.

## Compare the output to your real photo before you upload it

Compare the opening portrait with the gray-background edit above. The tortoiseshell glasses, closed smile, gold hoops, and hand under the chin remained recognizable. Some wisps of hair and freckles changed. Look at both images at equal size before deciding whether those differences matter for your headshot.

Use one prompt and one input to compare two tools. Record the image editor shown in the interface, the instruction you used, and whether you selected a region. If one editor offers an area selection, try the shared written instruction first and then add the selection as a separate attempt. This gives you a fairer basis for deciding whether the feature helped. A good result in one photo says something about that photo and edit.

If you need multiple portraits for a panel or team page, match the framing and background tone first. A shared crop rule makes the page feel cohesive even when everyone provides a different source photo. Asking a model to make every original look as though it came from the same photographer can introduce needless edits. Start with the smallest changes that give the headshots a consistent presentation.

## Frequently asked questions

![A circular preview helps check the space around the head and shoulders.](/uploads/blog/ai-prompt-for-professional-headshot/section-5.jpg)

*A circular preview helps check the space around the head and shoulders.*

**How can I change clothes without changing my face?** Begin from a clear original and describe a change below the neckline. Name the specific garment and ask to preserve your head angle, expression, and hair. Use the editor's selection tool if available. Compare the chin and hair edges with the original before saving. If the result changes your face, reopen the original and narrow the selected region or the request.

**How do I prevent a beauty-filter look?** Preserve the texture you can identify in the source, such as a few freckles or lines around the eyes. Ask for one visible correction such as a neutral white balance or a softer shadow. Avoid stacking skin cleanup, face reshaping, and dramatic lighting in the same request. Review the image again at the small size people will actually see on your profile.

**Can I use a selfie for a professional headshot?** Yes, when it is recent, sharp, naturally lit, and framed with room to crop. A selfie taken very close to your face can make near features appear larger. Try stepping farther from the camera and using a timer. That gives you more room around the shoulders and an easier starting point for wardrobe or background changes.

**Should I use Gemini or ChatGPT for my source photo?** Both officially support editing an uploaded image. Try your smallest intended change with the same original in each. Look for the version that best preserves your expression and important details. If you need to isolate an area, an explicit selection control can help specify where an edit belongs. Build your decision around your photo and the change you need.

## Conclusion

![Review the final crop at the size that people will actually see.](/uploads/blog/ai-prompt-for-professional-headshot/section-6.jpg)

*Review the final crop at the size that people will actually see.*

Check the finished image in the destination itself. What remains of the shoulders in the circular preview? Is the face large enough to see beside your name? Does dark clothing disappear into the background? If the photo is going on a speaker page, open a preview at desktop and phone widths. Save one full-size copy as well as the crop you upload so the image can serve a second format later.
