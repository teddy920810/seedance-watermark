## Quality checks for a product shot

Imagine an eight-second clip of an unbranded forest-green canvas tote on a light wooden table. The camera slides gently sideways. The required details are two handles, stable seams, the same green fabric and no new lettering.

For text-to-video, evaluate whether the result follows that description. Do not penalize a model for failing to reproduce a particular photograph you never supplied. For image-to-video, use the same approved, rights-cleared source photo and judge fidelity to that actual object.

Watch the full clip at normal speed before checking difficult frames. Inspect the handles during motion, the seam positions, the contact with the table and the background. A beautiful camera move fails the product brief if the bag acquires a third handle.

Separate delivery resolution from correctness. A larger export can preserve a wrong shape more clearly. Compare equivalent output settings where possible and document any mismatch. Avoid claiming that one family's maximum resolution proves better motion or product accuracy.

Decide the acceptance threshold in advance. If stable handles are mandatory, an otherwise pleasing clip with changing handles is rejected. This prevents the selection process from drifting toward whichever sample happens to look most dramatic.

## Control and sound are separate decisions
