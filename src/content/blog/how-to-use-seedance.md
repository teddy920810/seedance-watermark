Seedance is a family of AI video models from ByteDance. You may encounter it through ByteDance products such as Dreamina or CapCut, through a cloud service, or through a third-party interface. The controls, prices, export rules, and available model version can differ even when the same name appears at the top of the page.

This guide takes you from that first choice to a downloaded clip. It also shows where beginners tend to spend credits without learning anything useful.

## Use Seedance in six steps

1. Choose an access platform and confirm the model name.
2. Decide between text-to-video and image-to-video.
3. Write one shot with one clear action and camera move.
4. Select duration, aspect ratio, and quality for the intended platform.
5. Generate, review the clip while it is moving, and revise one variable.
6. Download the keeper and finish captions, audio, and timing in an editor.

## Seedance is a model rather than one universal app

ByteDance develops Seedance, but an access platform wraps the model in its own product. That platform decides which versions appear, which inputs you can upload, how many references you can attach, and how credits are charged. It may also apply its own moderation, queue, post-processing, watermark, and data-retention rules.

First-party access is useful when you want the workflow ByteDance currently supports. A third-party tool can be convenient when you want a simpler interface or several models in one place. Neither label tells you everything. Check the model version, privacy terms, export conditions, and support policy on the page you actually use.

Do not assume Seedances.co is the official ByteDance site. It is an independent third-party tool. The distinction should remain visible wherever access is discussed.

## Before you start

Prepare a ten-second job, not a movie. Write one sentence describing the shot you need, where it will be published, and what must stay accurate. For example: “A vertical six-second product reveal for Instagram; bottle shape and label must not change.”

Confirm that you have the right to upload the source image, face, logo, or client asset. Use a current browser, save an original copy of every input, and set a small credit budget for exploration. Note the date, model label, duration, ratio, and quality mode. These details make a successful result repeatable.

## Step 1 Choose text to video or image to video

Text-to-video is best for exploration. It can invent a location, subject, composition, and movement from a written description. Use it when you want options and can tolerate visual variation.

Image-to-video starts from a stronger visual anchor. Choose it when a face, character, product, or composition should remain recognizable. Use a clean image at the same approximate aspect ratio as the final video. Avoid tiny subjects, cropped hands, heavy compression, or text that must remain perfectly readable.

Some newer Seedance workflows accept several kinds of references. Only plan around those controls if they appear in your chosen interface. A tutorial recorded on another platform or model version may show inputs you do not have.

## Step 2 Write a prompt the model can direct

Build one shot from seven parts: shot type, subject, one action, setting, camera movement, lighting and style, and constraints.

*Medium shot of a ceramic artist at a workbench. She presses one thumb into a slowly turning clay bowl. The camera makes a gentle six-inch dolly-in. Soft north-window light, realistic documentary look, stable hands and face, continuous shot, no readable text.*

Concrete movement matters more than adjectives. “Presses one thumb into a turning bowl” gives Seedance something visible to perform. “Creates something beautiful with passion” does not.

If you use an image, stop redescribing every pixel. Say what moves, what the camera does, and what must remain unchanged. For more examples, use the complete Seedance prompt guide.

## Step 3 Choose the video settings

Model names and settings change, so treat the interface in front of you as the source of truth. In general, start with a short duration and a standard or fast quality mode. Longer clips give the model more time to drift. High-quality generation costs more without repairing a confused shot.

Choose **16:9** for standard YouTube and desktop video, **9:16** for Shorts, Reels, and TikTok, and **1:1** only when a square feed placement truly needs it. Compose for the final ratio from the beginning. Cropping a wide shot into vertical video can remove faces, products, and important movement.

## Step 4 Generate and review the moving result

Press Generate, then record how long the attempt takes. When the clip appears, watch it at normal speed at least three times. A paused first frame can look excellent while the face pulses, fingers merge, or a logo crawls during motion.

Review eight points:

- Does the main action happen in the requested order?
- Does the subject keep the same identity and clothing?
- Do hands, feet, wheels, and object contact move plausibly?
- Does product geometry remain stable?
- Is any generated text readable and correct? It often will not be.
- Does the camera perform one coherent move?
- Does audio match the scene when audio is supported?
- Do the first and last frames provide useful edit points?

Decide whether the clip is usable for its intended job. “Interesting” and “usable” are different standards.

## Step 5 Revise one variable at a time

Use three passes. In the **composition pass**, fix framing, subject position, and the starting image. In the **motion pass**, fix the action and camera. In the **finish pass**, refine light, texture, color, and audio.

If Seedance invents cuts, remove extra actions and locations. Add “single continuous take, no cuts.” If a face drifts, use a stronger identity description or a reference image. If movement is weak, replace emotional language with a physical action and direction. If a product bends, lock the camera and reduce interaction.

Generate two or three controlled variations rather than rewriting the entire prompt after every failure. Keep the same brief and change one line. Your notes will show what actually improved the shot.

## Step 6 Export and finish in an editor

Download the original file at the highest useful quality your plan permits. Keep the prompt and settings beside it. If the output needs more resolution, test a ten-second section in an upscaler before processing the full video. Aggressive enhancement can sharpen artifacts and create flicker.

Add precise text, captions, music, brand graphics, and final sound in an editor. Generative video is good at making shots; it is not always the best place to typeset a price, build a complete soundtrack, or control frame-accurate pacing.

## Is Seedance free

Free access may mean trial credits, a daily allowance, a slower queue, lower resolution, a watermark, or an export limit. Those terms can change by platform and region. Check them before starting a long sequence.

Measure the cost of a **usable** clip. If four attempts cost ten credits each and only one six-second result works, your cost is forty credits for six usable seconds. That number is more useful than the advertised price of one generation.

## Common Seedance problems and fixes

**The newest model is missing.** The version may not have reached your product, region, or account tier. Check the provider’s current documentation rather than forcing a tutorial’s workflow.

**The queue is stuck.** Save the prompt, refresh the job history, and avoid paying for duplicate clicks. Check the provider’s status or support channel.

**The ratio looks wrong.** Confirm the generation setting and the downloaded file dimensions. Some previews fit the clip inside a different-shaped player.

**The subject drifts.** Shorten the clip, simplify movement, repeat stable identity anchors, or switch to image-to-video.

**The prompt is rejected.** Remove ambiguous sensitive terms, copyrighted character requests, or unsafe content. Do not try to evade platform safeguards.

**The export has a watermark.** Check plan and export rules before generation. Remove only watermarks you own or have permission to remove.

**Generation is slow.** Test with a short clip or faster queue mode. Keep the job ID and avoid starting repeated copies unless the first request failed.
