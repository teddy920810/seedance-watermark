A handmade candle looks great in an AI-generated kitchen scene until you notice the lid has gained an extra ridge. Zoom in and the label says eight ounces where the jar says six. The image might still look like a professional shoot. It would give a customer the wrong idea about what arrives in the box.

Start with a photo of the item you actually sell. Ask for the lighting, surface and framing you want, then check the result against the original. If a label or shape has to match exactly, keep the photographed product on its own layer and generate only the surrounding scene. The examples below use a candle, a pouch and a ceramic mug. Replace them with a product and images you own or have permission to use.

## Give the tool a reference and a clear job

![Original six-by-four-inch digital postcard artwork. Its edition number, eight waves, and margins are reference details.](/uploads/blog/prompt-for-product-photography/section-1.jpg)

*Original six-by-four-inch digital postcard artwork. Its edition number, eight waves, and margins are reference details.*

For the postcard example pictured here, our reference was the original six-by-four-inch digital artwork. Look at the eight wave lines, the edition number, and the spacing above the small footer. Each gives you something to check when the card appears in a scene. For a physical item, take a straightforward reference before you start looking for a mood. Photograph the item from the angle you want to show, using a plain backdrop and enough light to see both sides of the label. For a jar or bottle, try a straight-on frame and a shallow three-quarter view. A straight-on angle makes the label easier to compare. A slight angle shows how deep the package is and how the cap sits above the body. If you also need to show the back of the package, photograph it separately.

An existing image gives the editor a product to work from. A generated scene described entirely in text is useful for trying layouts before a shoot. If the final image represents an actual SKU, upload the reference using the tool's image editing or reference controls and compare any candidate to the product before you post it. OpenAI's ChatGPT Images help documents editing by upload and written instructions. Its help also explains that edits can extend beyond the area highlighted in the editor. [OpenAI Images in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11084440-images-in-chatgpt)

A practical prompt specifies what the frame will be used for, the setting and source of light, the camera angle and crop, and the features to keep. Include a little extra space around a handle or cap so the final crop does not make the package look cut off.

**Copyable starting prompt**

> Edit the attached photo of my amber candle jar for a square product listing. Keep the real cylindrical jar, black lid, label artwork, existing text, glass color and front-facing angle. Set the jar on a seamless pale gray sweep with soft light from the upper left and a faint contact shadow. Center it with clear margins around the lid and base. Keep the entire item sharp and readable.

Pick an early version and put it beside the original at the same size. Try to see where the edit first departs from the source. If the upper edge gets thinner, look again at the crop and angle. If the label leaves out a line, the problem is product fidelity. That distinction tells you whether to revise the prompt or switch to a composite that keeps your photo intact.

## Write separate prompts for listing and lifestyle images

![Side-by-side prints illustrate a simple pouch listing and a window-lit ceramic mug scene.](/uploads/blog/prompt-for-product-photography/section-2.jpg)

*Side-by-side prints illustrate a simple pouch listing and a window-lit ceramic mug scene.*

For a listing, make the product easy to inspect. Use the same general angle from one SKU to the next and keep the camera level if you want the packages to look like a family. Keep props out of the space between the viewer and the label. When you ask for a white backdrop, describe a soft shadow below the object so it looks grounded. During review, compare how much of the frame the item occupies with the rest of your listings.

**Clean pouch listing**

> Use my photographed stand-up pouch as the product. Preserve the top seal, zipper line, side folds, printed label, and current shape. Show it upright and front-facing on a seamless off-white background. Add broad soft light from the left, with a short, light gray shadow touching the base. Keep a consistent margin and the full pouch in view.

A lifestyle image shows the product in a credible setting. Start from a use you can describe in one sentence. A ceramic mug on a desk beside an open notebook tells a different story from a mug on a crowded dining table. Ask for a few objects at believable distances. If you place a notebook behind the mug, keep it shallow in the frame so the handle and rim still tell the buyer what they need to see.

**Window-lit mug scene**

> Build a quiet breakfast setting around the attached photo of my ceramic mug. Keep the glaze color, speckled finish, rim, handle and proportions. Place it on a pale wood table next to one folded linen cloth. Let broad light fall from a nearby window on the left, with soft shadows going right. Frame it from just above tabletop height with the whole handle visible and open room above for a headline.

A close-up belongs in the set when the buyer needs to see how something is made. For a mug, photograph the glaze or handle joint directly and use the editor for crop, border or backdrop. A beautiful invented speckle pattern gives little useful information about the actual finish. Put the close-up and the main shot side by side and check that the color, surface and material feel like parts of the same object. Save the clean listing crop and the wider lifestyle crop as separate outputs so one can change without forcing revisions to the other.

## Protect the original label and approve the final image

![Actual AI scene made from the source postcard. Read the edition, then compare the wave curves and margins with the original.](/uploads/blog/prompt-for-product-photography/section-3.jpg)

*Actual AI scene made from the source postcard. Read the edition, then compare the wave curves and margins with the original.*

Compare the postcard artwork in the first section with the mockup above. We supplied our original digital artwork to OpenAI’s image editor in Codex and asked for the entire card on stone. The title, edition 06, and size label stayed legible. Count the lines at the left edge: all eight survived. Follow them across, though, and the curves sit differently against the small footer. Some letters changed weight. The card’s visible boundary measures about 1.58 to 1 in the image; the file is 1.50 to 1. Perspective can account for some difference, so the mockup cannot establish the card’s physical dimensions. For a downloadable print listing, place the actual artwork over the staged scene in perspective.

For a label that keeps changing, make the product from your original photo its own protected layer. Mask around it carefully and generate or edit the backdrop on a separate layer. Photoshop's desktop Generative Fill instructions describe selecting the area you want to change, writing a prompt and choosing a variation. Use a background-only selection in that workflow, then check the product layer and the mask separately. [Adobe Generative Fill in Photoshop](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/create-open-import-images/create-images/edit-images-with-generative-fill.html)

**Backdrop prompt for a composite**

> In the selected background, create a pale plaster wall above a smooth light wood counter at the same height and perspective as the source photo. Add broad window light from the left with soft shadows going right. Leave the surface around the product simple and keep the front area clear.

Once the background is in place, inspect the border. A halo around the neck of a bottle or formerly white pixels inside a mug handle can make the composite look pasted on. If the hand-painted label is already correct in your photo, avoid drawing it again. Adjust the mask until its edge follows the real silhouette and keep the genuine label pixels from the source.

If you plan a short clip later, use your approved still and shot notes to decide what a buyer should see first. A single slow move from the closed lid toward the printed label is easier to judge than a full spin that reveals unseen sides of the package. Check that the tool you plan to render in accepts the photo in the role you need. Seedances offers a text-to-video settings preview for drafting prompts, aspect ratio and duration choices. [Seedances text-to-video settings preview](/seedance-ai-generated)

Keep one approved export with the source photo and the original label artwork. For a second SKU in the same collection, reuse the lighting and margin instructions with a fresh photo of the new item. Then compare the two thumbnails side by side. This helps you keep the collection visually consistent while catching differences the buyer needs to see.

## Frequently asked questions

![A set of product photo crops shows how much of the jar remains visible at different sizes.](/uploads/blog/prompt-for-product-photography/section-4.jpg)

*A set of product photo crops shows how much of the jar remains visible at different sizes.*

**Why does the label keep distorting even when the prompt asks to preserve it?** A tool that generates new pixels can redraw small characters, even when the text names features to keep. Upload a larger, sharper reference and narrow the edit to the surrounding area. If the exact print matters, keep the original product layer on top and edit only the background. Check the export line by line against the physical package.

**Can a reference image lock the shape of my packaging?** A reference gives the model evidence about the package, but its output can still differ in cap height, edge shape or proportions. If the shape has to hold, composite the actual photographed item or keep those pixels in a protected layer. A close comparison with the physical item is especially useful when a subtle difference could make it look like another size or variant.

**How many photos should I shoot before using AI?** Start with a clean front image, the main angle you intend to post, and a separate detail photo for any important print or texture. Add a real back or side view if a buyer needs that information. Preserve consistent lighting and keep a master image before cropping. More angles are helpful when they reveal details viewers need to check.

**Which image should go first on a product page?** Choose the image that shows what arrives, with the complete product shape and readable identifying features. Use lifestyle images farther down the page to show context, scale or use. Check the cropped thumbnail beside its product title before publishing. If the rim or label disappears at small size, adjust the crop using the approved image.
