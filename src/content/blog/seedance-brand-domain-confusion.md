Think in three layers. The developer builds a model. A provider offers an interface or API, chooses supported settings and handles its own service relationship. A payment merchant bills the customer. Those roles may belong to related organizations, but you should identify the actual relationship instead of assuming they are interchangeable.

“Official” also needs an object. An official model announcement, an official help page for an independent service and an officially documented integration are not the same thing. Ask “official for which organization and which product?” before treating the word as an endorsement.

An independent provider is not automatically fraudulent. Equally, a polished interface, familiar logo or impressive demo does not prove licensing, backend authenticity or reliable support. The useful conclusion is specific: here is what this source establishes, and here is what still needs checking.

## Check the service behind the name

### Identity: follow the relationship, not the label

Record the exact hostname you intend to use, the service name in its terms, its support channel and the merchant shown before payment. If a site claims a developer partnership, look for corroboration from the developer or a documented integration relationship. A site's own marketing sentence is a different evidence level from a cross-linked announcement or contract.

Do not infer developer ownership from an account name containing “official,” a domain ending in .ai or a search position above the developer. Likewise, a missing link on one developer page is not enough to prove that every possible integration is unauthorized. If the relationship matters to a client project, obtain clarification before committing.

### Service: identify the actual generation you are buying

Write down the model label, generation mode, duration, resolution, audio setting and export conditions for the selected plan. Confirm whether a higher output resolution means native generation, a finishing operation or a separate paid step. A “Pro” subscription is a service plan, not necessarily a uniquely identified model endpoint.

If backend provenance is essential, request documentation suitable for your use case. Visual resemblance to a demo cannot establish the exact model used. Nor does a low price prove a substitute model. This review did not conduct the controlled generations or routing audit needed to make either claim.

### Terms: read the clauses relevant to your project

Check content handling, training permissions, retention, deletion, commercial-use conditions and third-party rights. A provider's statement that output belongs to you is not a guarantee that a recognizable character, person or soundtrack is cleared for every use.

For a concrete example, [seedance.ai's terms](<https://www.seedance.ai/terms>) include a user-content license that names training and optimization among its purposes. That wording is a reason to inspect the current clause and privacy settings before submitting client assets; it is not a finding about what happened to any particular upload. Ask for clarification where a confidential workflow requires stronger commitments than the public terms provide.

Before paying, distinguish monthly billing from an annual commitment displayed as a monthly equivalent. Confirm recurring charges, credit expiry, cancellation location, refund conditions and which features the selected plan includes. Do not convert a public pricing headline into an all-in project estimate without the selected settings and billing period.

## An ordinary user's domain-check scenario

Suppose a small bookstore wants a short video of an original illustrated reading nook. The owner has a public-domain-safe, self-created reference drawing and needs one moving shot, not a long subscription or an actor likeness. Here is a proposed decision exercise, not a transaction we performed.

Start by saving the intended domain and the exact product page. Compare the advertised mode with the task: image-to-video, one supported clip length, a defined aspect ratio and an export suitable for the planned edit. Keep the drawing offline until the provider's data terms are acceptable.

Next, collect the plan details in one note: merchant, payment period, credit unit, generation estimate, renewal date and cancellation route. If any essential field is unclear, stop before purchasing and ask the identified support team. “The model is popular” cannot fill a blank about the actual service contract.

Only after those checks should you consider a low-risk sample using nonconfidential, appropriately licensed material. Review fidelity to the drawing, unintended text, motion artifacts and export usability. Record the service, model label, settings and actual charges if you proceed. A good sample can inform a purchase decision, but it still does not independently prove hidden model routing.

The result is a practical choice between documented service offers, not a contest between two similar names. If neither offer supplies the information your project needs, postponing the purchase is a valid outcome.

## If you already paid the wrong site

First identify who actually billed you. Collect the receipt, exact website, account email, plan and transaction date privately. Use the merchant's authenticated account or verified support route to ask about cancellation and the applicable refund process. Do not send passwords, verification codes or full payment-card details in a support message.

The [FTC's phishing guidance](<https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams>) recommends contacting an organization through a website or number you know is real rather than using details in a suspicious message. Apply that principle to unexpected renewal or “account verification” emails. It is general security guidance, not an allegation about either named service.

Keep cancellation confirmation and support correspondence. Whether a refund is available depends on the relevant transaction, terms and applicable protections; this guide cannot promise one. An account on another Seedance-named site is not, by itself, evidence that the merchant you paid has cancelled your subscription.

## FAQ

### Is Seedance AI the same thing as Seedance 2.0?

Not necessarily. “Seedance AI” can mean the model family in ordinary conversation or a particular site's brand. Seedance 2.0 identifies a model version. In a purchase or support discussion, include the exact domain and plan rather than relying on those words alone.

### Is seedance.ai the official ByteDance model website?

Its current homepage describes an independent platform and disclaims ByteDance affiliation. Use ByteDance Seed's own model page for primary model information. This distinction says who is speaking; it does not provide a quality score for the independent service.

### Is seedance2.ai a newer model than seedance.ai?

The domain names cannot establish that. Our live access to seedance2.ai failed, and its indexed independence statement does not verify its current model menu. Confirm the actual model and plan with the relevant provider before comparing results or paying.

### Will my credits or login work across these websites?

Do not assume portability. This review established no shared account or credit arrangement. Confirm any transfer or bundled access with the actual operators, and avoid entering one service's password into an unrelated site's ordinary login field.

### Does “third-party” mean unsafe or fake?

No. It describes a relationship, not a verdict. Evaluate identity disclosures, service documentation, billing, data handling and support evidence. Neither an independence statement nor a recognizable brand name alone establishes the complete trustworthiness of a service.

### Where should I send a billing question?

Start with the merchant and account identified by your receipt, using a verified contact route. Do not assume the model developer handles a subscription sold by a separate website. If the purchase used another billing platform, follow that platform's documented process for the actual transaction.
