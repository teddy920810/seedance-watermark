The canoe was supposed to arrive at the dock. Instead, it came toward the lens and vanished in an abrupt cut. The model caught the mood in your description. The fog looked good. The yellow hull stood out. It just never had a clear route across the frame. That is a common place to lose a video idea when an entire scene is reduced to a handful of style words.

A cinematic AI video prompt gives the viewer somewhere to look and something to follow. Start with the opening composition, give the subject a path, and describe how the camera reacts. Add light and sound once the movement makes sense. The examples below follow a single lakeside scene from a vague draft to a prompt you can try in more than one model.

## Establish what the viewer sees first

![Wide and close compositions serve different moments in the same scene.](/uploads/blog/cinematic-ai-video-prompts/section-1.jpg)

*Wide and close compositions serve different moments in the same scene.*

Picture a short film about a paddler who arrives at a lake before anyone else. You need one usable shot to set the scene. The jacket and canoe matter because their colors help viewers follow the action against the water. The dock matters because it gives the action a destination. You can leave distant cabins and trees in the background without describing each one.

Decide how much of the scene appears in the first frame. A wide shot has room for the waiting dock and its approach. It invites the viewer to notice the relationship between the person and the lake. A close view of hands on the hull gives the viewer an intimate detail, but the direction of travel may be harder to read. Both serve a purpose. Choose the framing that does the first job in your edit.

Here is the sort of prompt that gives you a mood without much direction: “A beautiful dramatic film shot of a canoe at a foggy lake, artistic lighting, shallow depth of field.” The canoe could be empty or occupied, broadside or pointed straight at you. Shallow focus might obscure the dock that the next shot depends on. Before adding more adjectives, describe what should be recognizable on screen.

For a reusable starting point, follow one order each time. Name the first frame, subject, physical action, camera path, and endpoint. Then set the light and add sound when your mode supports it.

> Open on [framing and location]. [Subject] moves from [start] toward [destination]. The camera [move or hold] and settles on [landmark]. Keep [continuity details]. Use [light direction and color]. Add [one timed sound cue] if supported.

**Try this establishing prompt**

> A wide single shot from the lake shore before sunrise. A paddler in a red rain jacket approaches a weathered dock from the left, guiding a mustard-yellow canoe at the water’s edge. The dock sits on the right. Blue morning light gives the water a cool tone, and the canoe stays readable against it. Hold the opening composition long enough to establish where the paddler is going.

[Google’s video prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/video/video-gen-prompt-guide) explains how framing, composition, movement, and atmosphere contribute to a prompt. Names for lenses or film stocks are optional. If you use them, translate anything essential into a visible effect. “Keep the near corner of the dock sharp enough to see its edge” is easier to check than a phrase that describes how an image should feel.

## Give the camera and subject separate instructions

![A camera slider can track parallel to the paddler’s path beside the dock.](/uploads/blog/cinematic-ai-video-prompts/section-2.jpg)

*A camera slider can track parallel to the paddler’s path beside the dock.*

Imagine setting the camera on a slider parallel to the shore. Your subject moves left to right. The camera follows at a gentler pace and stops when the canoe lines up with the dock. It is a modest move, but it lets the surrounding boards and shoreline shift against the farther trees. That parallax can make a short clip feel grounded.

Write the camera move as its own sentence. Describe its path and make sure the framing can support the action. If the paddler crosses the whole picture while the camera moves the other way, the scene may feel hurried. If the canoe is already almost outside the frame when the shot starts, there is little room left for a tracking move.

**Try this version for controlled motion**

> Open in a medium-wide, waist-height view parallel to the shore. The paddler in a red jacket guides the yellow canoe from left to right until the bow reaches the corner of the dock. The camera slides right a short distance beside the paddler and settles on the bow. Keep both hands and the front half of the canoe visible. One continuous shot with a steady horizon.

You can use the same subject motion to compare two kinds of staging. In the first version, replace the camera sentence with “The camera stays fixed on the corner of the dock while the paddler enters the frame.” In the second, keep the sliding move. Ask two separate questions when you watch the output. Does the canoe follow the route you described? Where does your eye land when the camera settles? A usable result gives the action space and leaves you an endpoint you can cut from.

A pan turns the viewing direction from one place. A tracking move shifts the camera through space. If a model interprets “pan right” as a move through the scene, tell it where the shot begins and which landmark ends up centered. If you want parallax, mention an object close to the camera so its movement relative to the background is visible. [Runway’s text-to-video prompting guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/47313737321107-Text-to-Video-Prompting-Guide) offers examples of subject and camera movement in directed shots.

## Time the action and adapt the prompt across models

![Two frames show the timing of the approach and the arrival.](/uploads/blog/cinematic-ai-video-prompts/section-3.jpg)

*Two frames show the timing of the approach and the arrival.*

Watch the case lid for action and the table edge for camera movement. We inspected the silent previews from [Nano Banana Pro’s same-prompt test](https://nanabananapro.com/model-compare/video). Both models received text describing an earbud case opening on wet slate during a slow push-in. Kling 3.0 kept the lid closed through the first two sampled frames, then opened it with a modest change in scale. Seedance 2 opened the lid earlier and filled much more of the final frame. To keep the product small, specify the space you want around it at the end. The publisher’s previews demonstrate framing and timing. Audio was unavailable.

For a short shot, decide which part deserves a beat. The paddler approaches, slows, and steadies the canoe. That is enough. A wave to someone off camera, a match cut to a paddle, and a reveal of a cabin would add choices that compete with the arrival. If you need those actions, write a shot for each and assemble them later.

The lighting should give you a clue that stays steady while the action unfolds. In this scene, use the broad blue light above the lake and one warm dock light. Keep that warm reflection close to the boards, where it belongs. Sound can help sell the tiny motion when the canoe touches the dock. Check the controls for your chosen video mode first. Where audio is supported, describe a finite cue you can judge. Otherwise use the same note as a cue for post-production.

**Try this combined prompt**

> In one medium-wide shot at blue hour, a paddler in a red rain jacket guides a mustard-yellow canoe from the left toward a weathered dock on the right. Start with both the bow and the dock visible. In the first beat the paddler walks in. In the second the camera makes a short rightward slide and settles. At the end, the paddler steadies the bow with one hand. A small warm dock light leaves a soft reflection on the water while the rest of the scene stays cool. Audio if supported: light ripples during the approach and a quiet wooden tap when the bow reaches the dock.

To compare the canoe prompt in Runway and Veo, match duration and aspect ratio where possible. Note each model version. Mute both takes and check when the bow reaches the dock, whether the camera settles, and whether the jacket and hull keep their colors. Judge audio separately.

Keep the draft that preserves your critical shot. If the canoe reaches the dock but the camera overshoots it, change the settling instruction and leave the features that worked alone. An edit of “The camera stops moving as the bow reaches the dock corner and holds on that corner” gives you a new condition to check. Rewrite the whole prompt when the shot itself changes.

## Frequently asked questions

![Shot planning notes and a recorder help keep visual action and sound cues together.](/uploads/blog/cinematic-ai-video-prompts/section-4.jpg)

*Shot planning notes and a recorder help keep visual action and sound cues together.*

**Why do long prompts sometimes cause an abrupt cut?** Read the prompt as instructions handed to someone holding a camera. Every new location, framing, or major action can sound like a separate shot. For continuous movement, keep the setting, opening composition, camera path, and main action together. Try an explicit instruction to hold a single shot. When you actually want several angles, use separate prompts and put the cuts where they help tell the story.

**How do I write a sequence of cinematic AI video prompts?** List what the audience should learn from each shot. The wide lake view shows where the paddler is going. A close view of a hand steadying the bow shows that the canoe has arrived. A view low on the dock can introduce the sound of water and the next movement. Carry forward shared details like weather, costume, and direction of travel. Change the viewpoint and action as each shot requires.

**How should I adapt one prompt for a different video model?** Keep the scene, direction, and endpoint visible in both versions. Match the output settings when you can. Adjust only what the host needs, such as an audio toggle, a separate camera control, or a shorter duration. If a tool has just enough time for the approach, let the next shot show the canoe settling. This keeps the scene comparable without asking two different interfaces to behave identically.
