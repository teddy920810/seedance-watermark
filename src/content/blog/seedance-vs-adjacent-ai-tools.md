For **nano banana vs seedance**, choose Nano Banana when the required output is a still image or image edit; choose Seedance when you need a generated moving shot. Use them together when an approved image should guide a video. Neither label, by itself, describes the entire process of producing a finished explainer or advertisement.

That distinction also helps with adjacent tools—but it needs current qualifications. FLUX includes a video model, Midjourney offers image animation, and platforms such as HeyGen and InVideo can include Seedance. These are overlapping capabilities at different production layers, not seven mutually exclusive categories.

This guide uses official documentation and public interfaces reviewed on October 19, 2026. It is a task-selection comparison, not a paid generation benchmark. We did not measure image quality, video fidelity, latency or a completed automatic handoff between the tools.

## Quick decision: choose the deliverable first

Choose Nano Banana if the assignment ends with a product composition, concept frame, visual variation or edited image. Choose Seedance if movement, a camera change or an audiovisual scene is part of the actual deliverable.

Choose a broader production platform if the assignment also needs a script, presenter, captions, scene assembly and repeated project revisions. Choose neither generation model merely because the brief says “make content”: determine what file and editable project the recipient needs.

Google's current Nano Banana 2.1 model card lists image and text outputs. It can accept several input types, including video, but accepting video is not the same as generating a video file. Seedance 2.0's official description concerns joint audio-video generation with multimodal direction. [Nano Banana 2.1](<https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-nano-banana-2.1?hl=en>), [Seedance 2.0](<https://seed.bytedance.com/en/seedance2_0>)

## Nano Banana and Seedance compared on useful dimensions

Both need a defined brief, permitted source material and output review. Their main difference here is what you are asking them to produce—not a universal ranking of intelligence or realism.

| Decision dimension | Nano Banana image workflow | Seedance video workflow |

| Required output | A still asset or image edit | A moving shot, with audiovisual capabilities by version and route |

| Reference purpose | Guide or edit the image | Guide appearance, motion or another supported video input role |

| Handoff | Export the approved image and its brief | Export the accepted clip and generation notes for assembly |

For Nano Banana, record the actual model rather than treating the nickname as one specification. Google's documentation now includes Nano Banana 2.1 alongside other image models. Reference handling, output settings and editing behavior vary. Do not copy a limit from one tier into another. [Google image-generation guide](<https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/image-generation>)

For Seedance, record the version and serving platform. A model's documented capability does not establish that every website exposes the same controls. Nor does an approved still guarantee that the object remains unchanged throughout a generated clip.

The conditional verdict is straightforward: Nano Banana fits the still-image deliverable; Seedance fits the moving-shot deliverable. Whether either produces an acceptable result for a particular product remains a separate review question.

## Task boundaries and combinations: seven adjacent tools

### Nano Banana: prepare or revise the visual asset

Use the image stage to settle the product's shape, setting and framing before requesting movement. Export the chosen original image rather than a screenshot of it. Keep the brief and reference permissions with the file.

The boundary is output type. A text description of a camera move, or an image model analyzing a source video, is not a rendered moving shot. For a combined job, hand the approved frame to a supported image-to-video route and write instructions for what changes over time.

### FLUX: distinguish image models from FLUX 3 Video

“Seedance 2.0 vs FLUX” needs a model name. Black Forest Labs distinguishes FLUX.2 image generation/editing from FLUX 3 video. Its FLUX 3 Video release documents text-to-video, image/keyframe workflows, continuation and native audio, with clips up to 20 seconds. It therefore overlaps the video task directly. [BFL model families](<https://help.bfl.ai/articles/7655484417-what-flux-models-are-available>), [FLUX 3 Video release](<https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-video?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block>)

Use a FLUX image model as an asset-preparation option when appropriate. If evaluating FLUX 3 Video instead, compare matching shot requirements, settings and access routes against Seedance. Do not carry an image-model price or license over to a differently served video model.

### Midjourney: concept images and image animation

Midjourney is not image-only. Its official video guide describes turning an image into a five-second video using that image as the first frame, with animation and motion controls. That creates a real overlap with short image-to-video work. [Midjourney video guide](<https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video>)

You could explore an image in Midjourney and use its own animation path, or export a permitted still to another compatible video route. The controls are not automatically equivalent: Midjourney's guide notes that other image-reference types are not compatible with its video generations. Recheck what the video mode actually accepts.

### HeyGen: presenter and project-level production

For HeyGen vs Seedance, ask whether you need one shot or a presenter-led production. HeyGen's Video Agent guide describes script, visuals, narration, captions and scene revision, with further editing in AI Studio. It also documents an option to enable Seedance visuals. “HeyGen or Seedance” can therefore mean choosing a production workflow containing Seedance, not choosing between unrelated engines. [HeyGen Video Agent](<https://help.heygen.com/en/articles/12402907-how-to-get-started-with-video-agent>)

A possible combination is an authorized presenter segment plus a separately reviewed product insert. Keep presenter permissions, spoken claims and product details under review. A convincing synthetic performance does not verify the truth of the script or provide consent for someone else's likeness.

### InVideo: a platform route, not another Seedance version

InVideo's official guide explicitly offers Seedance 2.0 through its Generative models menu. It documents selecting inputs, resolution and duration, then confirming the credit cost. That makes “InVideo vs direct Seedance Pro use” partly a question of interface, available controls and billing. [InVideo Seedance guide](<https://help.invideo.io/en/articles/14755814-create-with-seedance-2-0>)

Define “direct” carefully: name the actual provider, model and account route. Compare what you can revise and export, not an assumption that the same model name implies identical settings, eligibility or pricing. Use only permitted inputs; do not disguise restricted material to evade a provider's safeguards.

### ComfyUI: connect operations and models

ComfyUI is a node-based workflow application. Its documentation describes connected steps that can create and save media, with workflows saved as JSON. It is an orchestration layer, not one competing foundation model. [ComfyUI workflows](<https://docs.comfy.org/basic-concepts/workflow>)

ComfyUI can suit a creator who needs explicit, repeatable asset-processing steps. But running the interface locally does not mean every node runs locally: Partner Nodes call external services and can require credits. Check the exact model, node dependencies and data destination. A workflow file does not grant access to closed model weights. [Partner Nodes](<https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/overview>)

### DeepSeek: plan, analyze and structure instructions

DeepSeek can occupy the writing and coordination stage: draft a script, separate dialogue from shot directions, or produce a structured review checklist. Its current API documentation supports chat responses and image-input content; it should not be described as text-input-only. However, that response interface is not a documented replacement for rendering a Seedance clip. [DeepSeek chat API](<https://api-docs.deepseek.com/api/create-chat-completion/>)

Treat its output as a draft requiring review. A script can contain invented facts, and an analysis of a frame does not establish continuity across a full video. Pass approved instructions to the generation or editing tool rather than claiming the language-model response is the finished media file.

## Build a small project around explicit handoffs

Consider a proposed product explainer for an unbranded orange enamel camping mug. The opening might be a presenter introduction, followed by a five-second product insert and a closing caption. This is a planning example, not a production we executed.

First, approve the script and its factual claims. A writing assistant can help organize it, but the owner must verify what is being said. Separate spoken lines from visual directions so a shot generator does not receive the entire project as one overloaded prompt.

Next, approve the mug asset. The required details are its orange body, white rim and single handle. An image tool can help prepare a composition; a real, permitted product photograph may be the better reference when exact appearance matters. Inspect the asset before making it the basis of motion.

Then choose the video route for the insert. Request one gentle sideways camera move and no added people, lettering or cuts. Check the whole clip for handle geometry, rim color and stable contact with the table. Reject a visually attractive result if it changes a mandatory product detail.

Finally, assemble the accepted elements in the chosen production environment. Add captions and timing deliberately; keep the presenter segment and insert independently replaceable. ComfyUI may help with a supported processing workflow, while a production platform may help with project assembly. Neither should be added simply to increase the tool count.

Keep a handoff record: approved script, original asset, exact generation mode, output settings, quote, accepted clip and revision notes. This makes a later replacement targeted: a caption correction should not force you to recreate a product shot unnecessarily.

## Compare total workflow cost, not unrelated prices

A language-model token, an image output, a second of generated video and a finished presenter minute purchase different things. Comparing their headline prices without their roles produces a misleading winner.

Budget each stage separately: preparation, still assets, moving shots, presenter or narration, assembly and revisions. Confirm whether reference inputs, failed attempts, higher-resolution delivery or a fixed membership fee affect the selected route. Subscription credits from different platforms are not a common currency.

For a small pilot, record the actual cost and time per accepted deliverable. Include review and repair. This guide establishes no cost-saving percentage: a planned combination can reduce unnecessary generation, but its real benefit depends on how much of the work passes your brief.

## FAQ

### Can a Nano Banana image become a Seedance starting frame?

Yes, through a compatible image-to-video route, subject to its input requirements and your asset rights. Export the original image and verify the selected mode; an ordinary reference slot may have a different role.

### Which should I use for a static product listing?

Start with the still-image task. Video generation is unnecessary if the destination displays only one image. Review product accuracy and text regardless of the image tool selected.

### Is Seedance 2.0 vs Midjourney always video versus images?

No. Midjourney also animates images. Compare its actual video controls with the Seedance route when the deliverable moves; compare image tools separately when preparing still assets.

### Can HeyGen or InVideo already include Seedance?

Their official guides document Seedance workflows. Check current account access, selected mode and quote. The platform's project controls are a separate purchasing decision from the underlying model.

### Does ComfyUI make Seedance free or fully offline?

No such conclusion follows from using ComfyUI. Execution depends on the selected nodes and models; external services can remain paid and remote even with a locally running interface.

### Should DeepSeek write my video prompt?

It can help draft and organize instructions. Review every factual claim and remove unnecessary directions before generation. A prompt draft is not proof that the final clip will follow it.

## Final verdict

Use Nano Banana for the approved image and Seedance for a suitable moving-shot task. Evaluate FLUX and Midjourney by their actual image or video model, HeyGen and InVideo by project and access needs, ComfyUI by workflow control, and DeepSeek by planning support. Build the smallest combination that produces the required, reviewable deliverable.
